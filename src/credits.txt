German practice — list credits

This learning deck contains 5,000 distinct German dictionary headwords with
short English translations. Inflected forms are consolidated, German spelling
and capitalisation are normalised, duplicates are removed, and common English
translations are simplified. Ordering uses aggregated word-form occurrences in
the German OpenSubtitles 2018 frequency list. This is a corpus-based ranking,
not a claim that one ordering is universal across speech, news, and writing.

Sentences 1000
This deck contains 1,000 distinct short German sentences and everyday
expressions. Ordering follows descending cleaned sentence occurrence counts
in OpenSubtitles2018. One-word entries, names, subtitle noises, bare noun
fragments and malformed lines are filtered out. This represents subtitle
usage; it is not a universal ranking of all real-life German conversation.
The simple English translations and contrasting wrong choices were authored
for this trainer. The current aim game displays only the correct translation.

Japanese Kana and Kanji

Kana: the 46 modern basic Hiragana and 46 modern basic Katakana. Romanized
sounds use the usual Hepburn-style spellings (shi, chi, tsu, fu). を / ヲ are
shown as "o (wo)": wo identifies the spelling, while o is the usual sound.
No voiced forms, small kana, combinations, or obsolete wi/we are included.
Reference charts: https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/learn-hiragana/
https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/learn-katakana/

Kana frequency counts: Chikamatsu, N., Yokoyama, S., Nozaki, H., Long, E.,
and Fukuda, S. (2000). A Japanese logographic character frequency list for
cognitive science research. Behavior Research Methods, Instruments, &
Computers 32, 482-500. Appendices B and C list character occurrence counts
in the 1993 Asahi Shimbun corpus: 20,711,361 Hiragana and 3,608,288 Katakana.
https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03200819
Author's copy:
https://researchmap.jp/YOKOYAMA_Shoichi/published_papers/622371/attachment_file.pdf
Only the basic characters' factual counts were transcribed; the paper and
its page images are not distributed by this website. ヲ has no listed count.
Each new local calendar day has all 92 characters once, then 408 repetitions:
204 Hiragana and 204 Katakana, apportioned by each script's observed counts
using the largest-remainder method. This gives 250 answers per script in a
500-answer day. A minimum weight of 1 handles an absent frequency count;
each character is covered in the opening 92 regardless of its repeat weight.
The order is shuffled deterministically for the day, so reload keeps the
current card. Extra rounds use weighted repetitions; daily totals are additive.

Jōyō Kanji: a curated KANJIDIC2 extract, downloaded 3 October 2026 (local
time), source file date 2 October 2026. Exactly 2,136 distinct characters
marked elementary-school grades 1-6 (1,026) or secondary-school grade 8
(1,110) are included. Name-only Kanji (grades 9/10) are excluded. English
labels use dictionary meanings, with brief labels and parentheses removed
where suitable; other meanings are retained to reject ambiguous distractors.
2,037 characters have the source's Mainichi Shimbun frequency rank; these
come first in rank order. The remaining 99 follow by grade and stable order.
Frequency ranks describe newspaper usage, rather than every type of Japanese.
https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/KANJIDIC_Project.html
Data: https://www.edrdg.org/pub/Nihongo/kanjidic2.xml.gz
Documentation: https://www.edrdg.org/kanjidic/kanjidic2_dtdh.html

KANJIDIC2 copyright: James William Breen and the Electronic Dictionary
Research and Development Group (EDRDG). This derived Kanji dataset is
distributed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0.
https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode
No endorsement by EDRDG is implied. Dictionary data is supplied without

Economics 3500

An original easy-to-hard trading and economics curriculum with 3,500 distinct
prompts: 720 terminology cards, 720 definition-to-term questions, 720 applied
concept scenarios, and 1,340 numerical questions spanning 100 calculation skills.
The opening 70 terms emphasize small-cap trading; the later topics broaden to
execution, risk, financial statements, valuation, portfolios, bonds, derivatives,
microeconomics, macroeconomics, monetary policy and international economics.
Ordering is curated by practical relevance and learning prerequisites. It is
not a measured frequency ranking of a universal list of economic items.

Definitions, scenarios and contrasting incorrect choices were written for this
trainer. Numerical problems use hypothetical amounts and explicitly stated
model assumptions. Unless another precision is given, numerical choices are
rounded to at most two decimals; m means million and bn means billion. Stop
levels are triggers, not guaranteed fills; planned-risk problems explicitly
exclude slippage and fees. No account-specific broker, tax or leverage rules
are assumed. Economics uses Monday-to-Sunday weeks in the browser's local time.
The weekly count and card position restart on Monday; replaying within a week
retains that week's totals. Earlier daily records are combined into week totals.

TRIVIA 20000

20,000 curated questions. Science, AI, history,
economics, geography, law, medicine, technology, culture, Greek and Cypriot
history, geopolitics, business, philosophy, engineering, astronomy, physics,
luxury and wealth, pharmacology, inventions, criminal justice, flags and
capitals receive the emphasis. Difficulty is an editorial estimate. There is
no claim that this is a statistically measured ranking of the 20,000 most
frequently asked questions. Some later questions require specialist knowledge.

Trivia's position and round accuracy carry across local midnights. Completing
all 20,000 unlocks REPLAY, which starts a fresh round while retaining dated
practice history. Progress is saved only in this browser.

of exact and similar duplicates, punctuation repair, estimated difficulty,
topical selection, and reduction to one correct and one incorrect alternative.

Flashcard practice — 8 October 2026

Flashcard games show their prompt and correct answer together. Space or Enter
advances one card, with no reveal step. Phones have a small Next Card button
at the bottom. It stays hidden on computers, and no keyboard hint is shown.
Language cards show English first and the target language below.
Aim targets, distractor choices and background-click scoring are removed.
Held-key repeats do not advance more cards. Menus, settings and history controls
do not count as practice. Skips count as ungraded practice, not correct answers.
Earlier correct/wrong and aim-miss records remain saved in the browser.
Text sizes stay constant between cards; longer text wraps instead of shrinking.

Arithmetic requires a typed answer and advances automatically only when it is
correct. Its original generator, operations, ranges, settings, 2,000-answer
weekly goal and Monday-to-Sunday history remain. Space and Enter cannot skip it.
ArithmeticAim has been removed from the menu and publication.
Earlier manually added totals and historical 1,000-card goals are preserved.
Earlier ungraded arithmetic practice remains in the saved totals.
The green cursor is shared by the game, menus and arithmetic interfaces.

Programming — 4 October 2026

5,000 original question-and-answer cards, progressing through ten difficulty
bands and 52 continuous course weeks. Weeks 1–8 have 97 new cards; weeks 9–52
have 96. The ten question perspectives for each of 500 authored micro-lessons
provide intentional repetition. The course starts at computer basics and
ends in practical extension, TTS, screener and desktop application engineering.
Subject counts match the requested percentages exactly. This is an authored
learning sequence, not a statistically measured ranking of popular questions.

Course data, the plan, counts and selected primary technical references are
available in programming/cards.json, programming/curriculum-plan.json,
programming/summary.json and programming/sources.json. The references check
technical facts; short examples and project scenarios are original.
Rebuild and review behavior are documented in scripts/programming-content.md
in the source checkout. Speech voices, browser capabilities and packaging
prerequisites depend on the target environment and are identified as such.

Programming also shows each answer immediately and advances with Space or
Enter. Its 5,000 cards continue in curriculum order across course boundaries.
Completed new cards never reset on a calendar rollover. Replay starts again
at card 1 while keeping total course completion. Earlier knowledge ratings,
review dates and mastery records stay saved; skips do not change those ratings.
All personal progress is kept only in this browser using a separate storage key.

QUANT — 8 October 2026

5,000 original question-and-answer cards, from 500 authored skills with ten
different teaching views each. Continuous difficulty progresses from simple
percentages and probability to combined professional quantitative reasoning.
Exact topic shares: statistics 30%, probability 15%, arithmetic 10%, EV/Kelly
and decisions 10%, papers 10%, calculus 10%, trading/betting/poker 7%, models
4%, product analytics 2%, information theory 2%. These are curriculum choices,
not a measured popularity ranking. Definitions and worked examples are original.
Selected primary/reference sources verify mathematics and research methods;
the source manifest, curriculum plan and counts are in quant/sources.json,
quant/curriculum-plan.json and quant/summary.json.

QUANT uses the current simultaneous question-and-answer style. Space or Enter
advances once, with no aiming, reveal, ratings or calendar resets.
Long text wraps at fixed sizes. Progress, practice history and per-card visits
use the separate browser-only quant5000:v1 key. Replay after 5,000 retains 100%
course completion. Ungraded practice cannot establish accuracy, weak topics
or mastery; these are shown as not rated rather than inferred from skips.
Review consists of authored recurrence across views and later applications.
